# Finish MedSwin before the Bedrock move

MedSwin 2.0 already abstains when its evidence gate fails, and that behaviour is the right clinical instinct. It is still strategically incomplete. The live process is one FastAPI service on Azure AI Foundry (`gpt-5.4`, Cohere `embed-v-4-0` at 1536 dimensions, `Cohere-rerank-v4.0-fast`), with hybrid retrieval, fusion, and a provenance check on the two-stage and full pipelines, and with no CDS Hooks cards, no FHIR Clinical Reasoning evaluation, no real JWT check, and no Bedrock client. Strategic completeness means four locked identities: an inspectable decision the clinician can verify, a vector space that matches the index, a control plane that can stop and resume without repeating a write, and Bedrock IDs that the docs and the Kollab catalog actually use. The cutover target is `global.openai.gpt-6-luna` for chat and `global.cohere.embed-v4:0` for embeddings, with rerank on `cohere.rerank-v3-5:0` under IAM, after the corpus is embedded again. This Mac (Apple M5 Pro, 48 GB) can hold MedEmbed-large-v0.1, bge-reranker-v2-m3, and the MedSwin 7B LLaMA line at the same time. It cannot host GPT-6 Luna. The regulatory sentences below are readings of published guidance. They are not a device-classification opinion.

## MedSwin abstains in prose and still misses the card contract

**Evidence.** The 24 November 2026 read-only audit of this repository shows one FastAPI process (`app/main.py`) plus MongoDB, a static clinician UI, and three pipelines over one corpus. Naive retrieval is dense top-k and always generates. Two-stage retrieval normalises, hybrid-retrieves, reranks, fuses, gates, and can retrieve again. The full pipeline adds in-process specialist claim agents. When the evidence gate fails after its loops, the orchestrator returns an abstention string and does not call synthesis. CDS Hooks 2.0.1 expects a different contract. A client posts to `{baseUrl}/cds-services/{service.id}` with `hook`, `hookInstance`, and hook-specific context, and a successful service returns HTTP 200 with a `cards` array. Each card needs `summary`, an `indicator` of `info`, `warning`, or `critical`, and a `source` label. The CDS client decides whether to show the card, auto-apply a suggestion, or accept an override. Empty cards are a valid “no guidance” result. Production traffic is JSON over HTTPS, FHIR access uses OAuth bearer tokens, and the security section calls for JWT authentication of the CDS service, a prefetch allowlist, freshness, and an audit of PHI use ([CDS Hooks 2.0.1](https://cds-hooks.hl7.org/STU2/)). FHIR Clinical Reasoning maps hooks to triggers and services to `PlanDefinition`, and it requires authentication on patient-specific evaluation plus logging of guidance ([FHIR Clinical Reasoning](https://hl7.org/fhir/clinicalreasoning-module.html)). MedSwin’s HTTP surface is `/api/v1/pipelines/{naive|two-stage|full}/chat` and related storage routes. The audit found no CDS Hooks discovery document, no card payload, and no `GuidanceResponse`.

The same gap shows up in the transparency rules that buyers and regulators already write down. FDA’s January 2026 CDS guidance keeps four cumulative Non-Device CDS criteria. The fourth requires the health professional to review the basis independently, including inputs, method, limitations, and the basis for the recommendation, with automation bias named as the risk ([FDA CDS guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)). TGA exemption for CDSS software is also cumulative: recommendations only to health professionals, no replacement of clinical judgement, no diagnosis or treatment decision by the software, and enough displayed guidelines, calculations, or logic that a professional can interpret and verify the output. Opaque systems need that displayed information. Exemption amendments from 1 November 2026 are described as clarity changes, with the exemption scope left as it was ([TGA CDSS guidance](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-clinical-decision-support-system-software-regulation), [TGA exemption amendments](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/news-articles/clinical-decision-support-system-exemption-amendments)). ONC’s HTI-1 rule replaced CDS certification with Decision Support Interventions under 45 CFR 170.315(b)(11): **13 source attributes** for evidence-based DSI and **31** for predictive DSI, including training data, fairness, external validation, and local validity monitoring ([ONC DSI fact sheet](https://www.healthit.gov/sites/default/files/page/2023-12/HTI-1_DSI_fact%20sheet_508.pdf)). In the EU, AI that is a medical device under notified-body MDR assessment is high-risk AI, and high-risk duties include risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, human oversight, and cybersecurity ([EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202401689)). NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework asks implementers to document knowledge limits and human oversight. It is voluntary ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)).

MedSwin already has pieces of the “basis” those texts describe, and it lacks the access control they assume. The gate accepts a bundle only when every required facet clears a lower confidence bound and an entropy cap, contradictions stay inside a cap, and every passage has a chunk id, a document id, a source type, and no weak provenance. Traces stored in Mongo hold messages, tool calls, retrieval, rerank, sufficiency, citations, and timing. The read API redacts PHI with regular expressions. `ENABLE_AUTH` defaults off. When it is on, the audit shows a shared-secret prefix check, and an empty `AUTH_JWT_SECRET` accepts any non-empty bearer token. `ENABLE_RBAC` is never read. `/health` stays open. That is not the JWT bearer model CDS Hooks describes, and it is not FHIR authorisation on patient evaluation. The product also has no wired drug–drug or drug–allergy check, no GRADE field on cards (there are no cards), and no ONC-style source-attribute record. A 2024–2026 systematic review of LLM clinical decision studies found weak external validation and calibration, and it treats benchmark accuracy as insufficient for clinical readiness ([PMC13585368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13585368/)). Accepted evaluation in the notes is a portfolio: BioASQ retrieval and ideal answers, with a **5,729-question** training set in the 2026 overview ([BioASQ 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39975)); vignettes that abstain when a case is undeterminable ([ClinDet-Bench](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-industry.47)); explicit abstention options ([MedAbstain](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.291.pdf)); false-premise items whose correct action is abstain ([AdverseMed-500](https://huggingface.co/datasets/calnugget/adversemed-500/blob/main/README.md)); and calibration, coverage, and harm ([BASICS-CDSS](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23133644)). MedSwin’s system eval is a 3-pipeline by 2-generator contract. It does not run that portfolio. No single regulator-approved LLM-CDSS benchmark is identified in the notes.

**What to change.** Keep the abstention. Treat an insufficient gate as an empty card or an explicit insufficient response, which CDS Hooks already allows and which the abstention benchmarks reward. Add a thin CDS Hooks 2.0.1 adapter that maps a passed bundle to a card with `summary`, `indicator`, and `source`, and that maps a failed gate to an empty `cards` array or a warning card whose detail is the missing facet, the contradiction count, and the passage ids. Put the passage ids, source types, and the gate’s actual predicates in the detail a clinician can read, which is the practical reading of FDA criterion 4 and TGA criterion (e). Turn on authentication that verifies a JWT, close chat when auth is off in any shared deployment, and stop treating a secret prefix as a signature. Log the card id, the hook instance, the clinician, the passage ids, and the override, and keep that log as the record. Add the evaluation portfolio above as regression gates. Do this before describing the service as a production CDSS. A prose chat box with a disclaimer does not meet the card contract, and the EU literature cited in the notes treats disclaimer-only labelling as a weak reading of oversight duties ([Frontiers MDR/AI Act review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1932256/full)).

## Named thresholds and named models do not steer the live path

**Evidence.** The gate’s real predicates are facet lower confidence bounds, entropy, contradiction count, provenance, and marginal utility. `SUFF_T_INCLUSION` and `SUFF_T_MEAN_CONF` are range-checked at startup and never compared in `EvidenceGate.decide`. `SUFF_T_CPG` and `SUFF_T_EMR` only scale reported kappa values. Changing those four settings does not change answer, retrieve-more, or insufficient, except that an out-of-range inclusion or mean-confidence value fails startup. `PipelinePolicy.generation_token_budget` is set and never read. Packing uses `TOKEN_BUDGET_B`, default **1800** evidence tokens. Fusion validation sums six weights, while scoring also applies evidence-based-medicine, noise, and safety-reward terms. `DEFAULT_INDEX_TYPE`, `INDEX_STRATEGY_MODE`, `ENABLE_OTEL`, and the AWS region settings are assigned in config and unused by the application. The tree index is built in code and never queried. `lab/` is a second stack that `app/` does not import.

The retrieval models named in config are not the models the live process uses, and they are not interchangeable with each other. The live cloud embedder is Foundry `embed-v-4-0` at **1536** dimensions. The configured local path is `./models/MedEmbed-large-v0.1`, and `EMBEDDING_DIMENSION` stays **768**. The startup download, which cloud mode skips, fetches `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` into that MedEmbed path. The existence check requires `pytorch_model.bin` and does not accept `model.safetensors`, so a safetensors-only directory is treated as missing and can be overwritten. Local mean-pooling of the last hidden state is what the embedding manager implements. The canonical MedEmbed checkpoint is a different object. `abhinand/MedEmbed-large-v0.1` is a **335,141,888**-parameter BERT bi-encoder, fine-tuned from `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5`, with **CLS pooling, L2 normalisation, 1024 dimensions, and a 512-token maximum**, under Apache-2.0. On-disk size is about **1.34 GB**. Its card reports **nDCG@10 of 52.899** on CUREv1 English ([MedEmbed model card](https://huggingface.co/abhinand/MedEmbed-large-v0.1), [MedEmbed config](https://huggingface.co/abhinand/MedEmbed-large-v0.1/raw/main/config.json), [pooling config](https://huggingface.co/abhinand/MedEmbed-large-v0.1/raw/main/1_Pooling/config.json), [MedEmbed README](https://huggingface.co/abhinand/MedEmbed-large-v0.1/raw/main/README.md)). A third-party host’s “32k context” label for the same name contradicts that config and is not used here. The configured reranker path is `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3`, a **567,755,777**-parameter XLM-RoBERTa cross-encoder fine-tuned from `BAAI/bge-m3`, Apache-2.0, with `model.safetensors` about **2.27 GB**. Config `max_position_embeddings` is **8194**, and the maintainer describes **8192** as the practical maximum. The score is a classification logit. FlagEmbedding’s `normalize=True` applies a sigmoid ([bge-reranker-v2-m3](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3), [reranker config](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3/raw/main/config.json)). The live cloud reranker is `Cohere-rerank-v4.0-fast` on the Foundry host. Any rerank exception, including a long HTTP 429, returns the original order and marks calibration `identity:rerank-error`.

The generator name is a third collision. Config and the live `.env` set `MEDSWIN_MODEL_REPO` to `MedSwin/MedSwin-DaRE-TIES-KD-0.7`. The API does not download that repo at startup. Local generation runs only when `GENERATION_BACKEND` is the local backend, through `scripts/serve-medswin-llm.py`. On Hugging Face, the MedSwin organisation’s generative line is a **7B English medical LLaMA**, base `medalpaca/medalpaca-7b`, architecture `LlamaForCausalLM`. `MedSwin/MedSwin-7B-SFT` reports **6,738,423,808** parameters and about **13.5 GB** of BF16 weights, Apache-2.0, and is labelled research CDSS with human review required ([MedSwin-7B-SFT README](https://huggingface.co/MedSwin/MedSwin-7B-SFT/raw/main/README.md), [MedSwin-7B-SFT API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/MedSwin/MedSwin-7B-SFT), [medalpaca-7b config](https://huggingface.co/medalpaca/medalpaca-7b/raw/main/config.json)). Sibling checkpoints include a distillation from `google/medgemma-27b-it` and the DaRE-TIES merge this repo configures ([MedSwin org](https://huggingface.co/MedSwin)). IEEE papers that use the title MedSwin describe a Swin Transformer encoder for 3D CT segmentation. Those papers are a different artefact ([ICAISS 2025 MedSwin](https://doi.org/10.1109/icaiss61471.2025.11042017)). No Swin-encoder-plus-LLaMA checkpoint was found under the MedSwin Hub organisation. The published context length of the DaRE-TIES merge itself was not opened. The local server fails closed when the prompt plus the completion exceed the loaded context window, and the completion cap defaults to 1024 new tokens. Ordinary cloud calls are uncapped unless `LLM_DEFAULT_MAX_TOKENS` is set.

Two further identity bugs sit on the request path. `GENERATION_BACKEND=medswin` is a local alias inside the chat client, while `/pipelines/ready` compares only the raw string `medswin_local` and otherwise reports the Foundry model. With cloud mode on, ready can report `gpt-5.4` while chat calls the local MedSwin id. `GENERATION_BACKEND=foundry` with cloud mode off posts the Foundry model name to the supervisor URL. The live `.env` sets both cloud flags, so that second case is not the current process. Operator index builds post an `org_id` that the build schema drops, and the handler builds a global HNSW index with `org_id=None`. Startup does not compare the index dimension with the active embedding dimension before `/health` can look healthy. Cloud health does check index provenance through storage stats. Dense exact scan drops vectors whose length differs. A Foundry 1536-d index and a local 768-d setting, or a true MedEmbed 1024-d index, are different spaces.

**What to change.** Make the gate’s environment names match `decide`, or delete the names that do not vote. The predicates a clinician is shown have to be the predicates that accept or abstain. Point the local download at `abhinand/MedEmbed-large-v0.1`, accept safetensors, pool with the CLS head, L2-normalise, and set the index to **1024** dimensions. Chunk embedder inputs at **512 tokens**. Call `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3` only on a small top-k, because cross-encoder cost scales with the number of pairs and the length of each pair, which is the hybrid-plus-rerank pattern BAAI describes ([bge-m3 model card](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-m3)). Keep Cohere cloud vectors in their own index. Do not query a 1536-d HNSW index with a MedEmbed vector. Refuse `/health` when the manifest dimension disagrees with the active embedder. Pass `org_id` into the build so one organisation’s index is not the global ANN. Calibrate fusion to one rerank scale. A Cohere relevance score and a bge logit are not the same number, and the fail-open path already labels itself `identity:rerank-error` when it skips the reranker. For the generator, serve `MedSwin/MedSwin-DaRE-TIES-KD-0.7` only from weights you placed on disk, and make `/pipelines/ready` use the same backend alias as the chat client. Smoke-test retrieval on a public IR set. MedEmbed’s own CUREv1 nDCG@10 is the regression anchor. CUREv1 is a **2,000-query**, 10-domain literature retrieval set under CC BY-NC 4.0, and its authors position it as evaluation of literature retrieval ([CURE](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06954)). A published BERTScore of **0.8396** on the MedSwin-7B-SFT card is a generator regression signal in the same spirit. Neither number is a clinical-validity claim.

## Five specialists share one process and no durable memory

**Evidence.** There is no supervisor that can cancel a specialist. In this code, `supervisor` is the chat client used by the query normaliser and the synthesis agent. On the full pipeline the first loop runs emr, guideline, safety, quality, and critic in sequence. Later loops add agents for missing facets. Two-stage skips those claim agents. A claim-agent exception returns `degraded=True` and zero claims, with no second model call. The retrieve loop stops on a passed gate, on a disabled retrieve-more flag, or when the gate says another loop will not help, and it is capped by `MAX_RETRIEVE_LOOPS` (default 3). Token counts returned by the provider do not stop the loop. Full and two-stage trace writes are not wrapped. A Mongo failure after a good answer becomes a generic error. Naive persistence is wrapped. `ENABLE_OTEL` is unused.

Durable engines work differently. Temporal records an event history and re-executes workflow code on resume, substituting completed activities. Activities are at-least-once unless `maximumAttempts` is 1, which allows zero executions. Idempotency is the downstream system’s job. A stable key is a business id, or workflow run id plus activity id, and it must not be the retry count ([Temporal workflows](https://docs.temporal.io/workflows), [Temporal activities](https://docs.temporal.io/activity-definition)). Inngest memoises `step.run` by a stable step id and re-runs code outside the step. The default is four retries after the first attempt. A success lost before it is recorded is sent again ([Inngest steps](https://www.inngest.com/docs/learn/inngest-steps), [Inngest best practices](https://www.inngest.com/docs/durable-execution/best-practices)). LangGraph checkpoints a super-step under `thread_id`. `interrupt()` pauses until a resume value arrives, and the interrupted node starts again from its first line, so side effects before the interrupt run again. The documented safe place for a human decision is inside a tool, before the API or database write, with a fresh read of external state after a long pause ([LangGraph interrupts](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/interrupts)). The OpenAI Agents SDK will run local shell and patch tools without approval unless `needs_approval` is set, and that approval is not a sandbox ([OpenAI Agents tools](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tools/)). Anthropic’s agent guidance distinguishes fixed workflows from agents that choose their own tools, prefers a fixed path when the subtasks are known, and treats a maximum iteration count as a normal stop. The page also says the December 2024 tooling advice has been partly superseded ([Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)).

MedSwin’s evidence pack is already a bounded working set: 1800 tokens, passage ids, and a gate. What it does not have is the three-layer memory the current compaction literature implies. CompAct-Bench took **200** tasks that had been solved before compaction. Mean accuracy after compaction was **56.7%**. The best reported overall score was Claude Opus 5 at **68.5%**. Premature commitment showed up in **56.7%** of early failures and **46.0%** of budget-exhausted cases. Critical omission was associated with an **18.2-point** increase in repeating finished work. Most failed continuations had at least one compaction error (**92.5%** of early failures, **85.8%** of budget exhaustions). Prefix length inside a domain was essentially uncorrelated with strict accuracy ([CompAct-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25673v1)). Compact-Bench found LLM summaries retaining at most **16.0%** of recurring details, against **79.1%** for mechanical extraction ([Compact-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31710)). Microsoft’s CompactionBench preprint, on **13.5 million** Copilot sessions, found that **7.8%** of sessions compacted and those sessions accounted for **44.2%** of tokens. Synchronous summarisation delayed the agent and cut prefix-cache reuse. Their append-only summary method reported up to **25.4%** lower total cost and **9.6%** lower execution time ([CompactionBench preprint](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CompactionBench_preprint.pdf)). Letta’s own split is the practical design: a bounded core block that stays in the prompt, a searchable recall log of what was said, and an archival store that is not filled automatically when the window overflows ([Letta memory blocks](https://docs.letta.com/v1-sdk/memory/memory-blocks), [Letta archival memory](https://docs.letta.com/v1-sdk/memory/archival-memory/index.md)). OpenAI’s server-side compaction item is opaque. A CDSS cannot audit that blob as the chart ([OpenAI compaction](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/compaction)). No clinical note-compaction study with omission rates for medicines, allergies, or labs was found. No primary source in the notes assigns retrieval, rerank, sufficiency, and generation to named clinical roles. Corrective RAG’s lesson, which does transfer, is that an all-irrelevant retrieved set is a path to fabricated facts, and that a prompted chat model was a weaker relevance judge than a fine-tuned evaluator on their comparison ([CRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884)). A 2026 reproduction argues that the T5 evaluator behaves more like an entity-alignment detector than a semantic judge ([CRAG reproduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16169)).

Prompt caching is exact-prefix reuse of KV state. Tool definitions sit in that prefix. A change at or before the breakpoint, a model change, or a rewritten history drops reuse from that point. For GPT-5.6 and later on OpenAI’s API, cache writes cost **1.25×** input for a 30-minute life, or **2.5×** for a 12-hour minimum in the Responses API. Reads cost **0.1×** on most of those models. The minimum cacheable visible prefix is **1,024** tokens. Explicit mode with no breakpoint does not cache ([OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). Anthropic’s default TTL is **5 minutes**, refreshed on use, and a tool definition change invalidates tools, system, and messages. A run of tool calls that outlives the TTL pays the cache-write price again ([Claude prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). MedSwin’s cloud calls are stateless HTTP. They do not pin a prefix, pass reasoning items back, or record `cached_tokens`. The rate limiter makes a first HTTP 429 on chat or embeddings sleep at least **600 seconds**, capped at 1800, even when `Retry-After` is shorter. Rerank fails open after 120 seconds. Connect errors retry once, which is the right local-port behaviour. A clinical call that is merely busy can still sit for about ten minutes.

**What to change.** Keep two-stage as the fixed workflow. Anthropic’s guidance prefers that when the subtasks are known: retrieve, rerank, judge sufficiency, then generate. Keep the full pipeline’s specialists as tools that return claims with passage ids. The manager keeps the stop budget and the final structured output. A handoff that gives a specialist the whole thread makes the stop harder. Do not let the generator hold a write tool. MedSwin does not write orders today. If a later version proposes a chart update, pause on a durable interrupt, re-read the record, and send the write with an idempotency key that is the run id plus the action, not the attempt number. Cap model turns, tool calls, and no-progress loops in the orchestrator. The retrieve cap already exists. The provider’s token count does not yet participate. Persist the trace before the HTTP response is committed, including on the full pipeline. Store three layers: the current bundle and a small structured working set (identifiers, medicines, allergies, problems, and whether each fact is ordered, resulted, or only reported), the raw passages addressable by id, and a summary that only points at those ids. Extract the fields CompAct-Bench shows summaries distort: exact identifiers, numbers and units, negation, tentative versus established, and work that already finished. Use a schema for that extraction. The Compact-Bench gap between mechanical extraction and LLM summaries is the reason. Leave OpenAI’s opaque compaction item out of the legal record. For cache, when the host supports it, put stable tool schemas and instructions first and the case text last. Do not sort tools per request, and do not put a timestamp in the cached prefix. Measure cache hits. A stable-looking prompt is not evidence of a hit. Replace the 600-second floor with a backoff that honours a short `Retry-After` and that surfaces throttle counts. Kollab already does the visibility half of that. Copy the policy. Do not copy a ten-minute stall into a bedside call.

## A Foundry hostname swap will not land on Bedrock Luna

**Evidence.** Application code has no Bedrock client, no `boto3`, and no `bedrock-runtime` URL. `requirements.txt` does not depend on `boto3`. Chat is `httpx` to `{Foundry endpoint}/openai/v1/chat/completions` with an `api-key` header and model `gpt-5.4`. The client omits `temperature` and sends `max_completion_tokens` only when the model id starts with `gpt-5`. Any other id, including a GPT-6 id, gets `temperature` and `max_tokens`. Embeddings go to Foundry’s embeddings route or, if forced, to a Cohere-native body. Online calls default `input_type=query`. Corpus writers must pass `document`. Rerank posts a Cohere v2 payload to `{account}/providers/cohere/v2/rerank`. `aws/deploy.sh` is a legacy EC2 rsync. The Kollab tree is a different repository. It is not imported here.

GPT-6 Luna is an active Bedrock model. Launch date **22 September 2026**. End of life no sooner than **22 September 2027**. Context window **1,050,000** tokens. Maximum output **128,000** tokens. Knowledge cutoff **18 May 2026**. The model accepts text and images and returns text. The base model ID is `openai.gpt-6-luna`. The bare string `gpt-6-luna` is not a Bedrock model ID. On `bedrock-runtime` the caller uses `us.openai.gpt-6-luna` or `global.openai.gpt-6-luna`. In-region `bedrock-runtime` is not supported for Luna. Mantle is `us-east-1` only, with model `openai.gpt-6-luna` and base URL `https://bedrock-mantle.us-east-1.api.aws/openai/v1`. The runtime OpenAI base URL is `https://bedrock-runtime.{region}.amazonaws.com/openai/v1`. Sample calls use `model="us.openai.gpt-6-luna"`. Reasoning effort values are `none`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, and `max`, default `medium`. Service tier is Standard only. On runtime, each output token counts as **10** quota tokens. Exact account RPM and TPM were not on the card. Short context is **272,000** input tokens or fewer. Global cross-region prices per million tokens are input **$0.10**, cache write **$0.125**, cache read **$0.01**, output **$0.50**. Mantle and US geographic inference add a 10 percent premium. Above 272,000 input tokens the higher rate applies to the whole request. Global long-context output is **$0.75** per million tokens. Runtime feature prose names Guardrails, structured outputs, and implicit prompt caching on Responses, Chat Completions, InvokeModel, and Converse. Explicit prompt caching is named for Responses, Chat Completions, and InvokeModel, and is not supported on Converse. Knowledge Bases are not supported on Luna runtime. Converse rejects a document block. `ap-southeast-2` can call the global profile. That profile is global cross-region inference. A source region in Europe is in the same position on the card: global profile only, not an EU geographic profile ([GPT-6 Luna model card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-openai-gpt-6-luna.html)). AWS announced GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol as generally available on **17 November 2026**. Those models do not replace the Luna ID ([AWS what’s new, 17 November 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/openai-gpt-6-2-astra-sol-amazon-bedrock/)).

Cohere on Bedrock is a different wire protocol from Foundry embeddings. The embed ID to send is `cohere.embed-v4:0`. A parameters sentence also writes `cohere.embed-v4` without `:0`. The model card, the sample, the CDK identifier, and the guardrails table use `cohere.embed-v4:0`. Geo IDs are `us.cohere.embed-v4:0` and `eu.cohere.embed-v4:0`. The global ID is `global.cohere.embed-v4:0`. `input_type` is required: `search_document`, `search_query`, `classification`, or `clustering`. `output_dimension` is **256, 512, 1024, or 1536**, default **1536**. Maximum **96** texts per call. v3 English and multilingual IDs are `cohere.embed-english-v3` and `cohere.embed-multilingual-v3`, with **1024**-float vectors and a **512**-token input. v3 `truncate` uses `START` and `END`. v4 uses `LEFT` and `RIGHT`. Embed calls are `InvokeModel`. Streaming embeddings are not supported ([Embed v4 model card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-cohere-embed-v4.html), [Embed v4 parameters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-parameters-embed-v4.html)). The documented rerank call is the `Rerank` API on `bedrock-agent-runtime`, model ARN `arn:aws:bedrock:{region}::foundation-model/cohere.rerank-v3-5:0`, one text query and 1 to 1000 sources ([Use a reranker](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/rerank-use.html)). Geo and global inference for Rerank 3.5 are not supported. Lifecycle is Active. The “no sooner than 2 December 2025” field is a floor, and the card still says Active ([Rerank 3.5 card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-cohere-rerank-3-5.html)). Bedrock API keys cannot call Agents runtime. Rerank therefore needs IAM if you follow the published Rerank API ([API keys](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/api-keys-use.html)). Whether `InvokeModel` can call `cohere.rerank-v3-5:0` directly was not shown on the pages fetched. Embed v4’s “no sooner than 15 April 2026” floor has also passed, and the card still says Active. Do not treat that date as a completed retirement, and do not treat the model as newly guaranteed past it.

Kollab, under decision D-58, uses Bedrock as the only routed model plane and IAM SigV4 through the boto3 default chain. Static Bedrock API keys are removed. Chat is `httpx` POST to `https://bedrock-runtime.{region}.amazonaws.com/model/{modelId}/converse`, signed as service `bedrock`. The Luna catalog id is `global.openai.gpt-6-luna`, with `reasoning.effort` and with temperature omitted (`sampling_temperature=False`). Embeddings are boto3 `invoke_model` on profile `bedrock-cohere-v4`, model `global.cohere.embed-v4:0`, `output_dimension` **1536**, batch max **96**, and a hard length check that declines the batch on mismatch. `input_type` is `search_document` or `search_query`. The response shape Kollab expects is `embeddings.float`. Region resolution prefers `AWS_BEDROCK_REGION`, and the embed default in that tree is `ap-southeast-2`. Rerank in Kollab is also `invoke_model`, body `api_version` 2, default model `cohere.rerank-v3-5:0`, and an allowlist of `ap-northeast-1`, `ca-central-1`, `eu-central-1`, `us-east-1`, and `us-west-2`. Sydney is not on that list. Production rerank is IAM. A bearer token exists only for local smoke when the database type is local. Embed retries are application-owned, six attempts, connect timeout 5 seconds, read timeout 60 seconds. Converse maps 401 and 403 to auth, 429 to rate limit, and splits parameter 400s (a bad temperature) from wire-schema 400s so one bad candidate does not kill every sibling. Backoff can be disabled, and a 429 still honours `Retry-After` up to 30 seconds. Tests cover SigV4, emit-tool structured output, embed batching, and a live IAM probe. Those tests live in the Kollab tree, not in MedSwin.

**What to change.** Copy Kollab’s split, not its whole fleet. Chat and embeddings use IAM. Put secrets in the SDK credential chain or in the environment, never in source. Prefer short-lived role credentials. A long-term Bedrock API key is documented for exploration, and it still cannot call the Agents rerank API. For Luna chat, follow Kollab’s catalog string `global.openai.gpt-6-luna` when the compute region is `ap-southeast-2`, and omit temperature the way Kollab does. MedSwin’s `gpt-5*` special case will send temperature to any GPT-6 id and will get the parameter 400 Kollab already classifies as a candidate fault. Set an output cap. Luna allows 128,000 output tokens, and MedSwin’s ordinary calls are uncapped. Keep reasoning effort explicit. Default on the card is `medium`. Use Chat Completions on `/openai/v1` if you need the smallest move from today’s Foundry body and you need explicit prompt-cache breakpoints. Use Converse, as Kollab does, if you want one message schema and structured output through an `emit` tool. Converse on Luna does not take document blocks, and the Luna card does not list explicit prompt caching on Converse. Implicit caching is named there. Kollab’s `cachePoint` on a Converse system block is a pattern for models whose Converse API honours it. For Luna, do not assume that breakpoint is explicit cache. Record `cacheReadInputTokens` the way Kollab records usage, and treat a missing hit as a miss.

Rebuild the index. Foundry `embed-v-4-0` at 1536 and Bedrock `global.cohere.embed-v4:0` at 1536 share a dimension and a vendor family. The notes do not establish that the vectors are equal. The enums differ (`query` versus `search_query`, `document` versus `search_document`), the JSON body differs, and the batch cap drops from Azure-style limits to **96**. Point queries at `search_query` and corpus rows at `search_document`. Decline a batch whose vectors are not length 1536. Re-embed. Then build HNSW from that space. Do not leave a Foundry index in front of a Bedrock query vector.

Rerank is the sharp edge. The live model is Cohere rerank v4 fast. The Bedrock ID in both the AWS card and the Kollab default is `cohere.rerank-v3-5:0`. That is a model change, so fusion weights and `p_hat` need a new calibration, and the fail-open identity path should stay visible in the trace. Kollab calls `InvokeModel`. The AWS user guide’s application path is `Rerank` on the agent runtime, which needs IAM and is unavailable to a bearer key. Those two shapes were not shown to be equivalent in this research. Implement the documented `Rerank` API with IAM unless a live probe in your account shows Kollab’s `InvokeModel` body succeeding. Run that call in an allowlisted region. `ap-southeast-2` is Kollab’s chat and embed default and is absent from Kollab’s rerank allowlist, and the rerank card has no global profile. A Sydney stack can call Luna and Embed v4 through global profiles, and those profiles are cross-region inference. It cannot keep rerank in Sydney on the evidence here. Say that data path in the privacy note. Global Luna is not an Australia-only geographic profile. The notes do not establish whether that profile meets a specific Australian hosting rule.

Copy Kollab’s error taxonomy and backoff: six attempts on embed, transient 429 and 5xx only, `Retry-After` respected up to a short cap, candidate-scoped 400s, and a single production embedding profile. Do not copy the local rerank bearer, the Mantle path for other vendors, retired `gpt-5.6` strings, or a hot JSON kill switch until MedSwin has more than one routed chat model. Leave GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol as later catalog entries. They are newer. They are not the Kollab Luna id this migration is built on. Add `boto3`. Delete the assumption that `AZURE_AI_FOUNDRY_API_KEY` and `api-version=2024-05-01-preview` travel with the call. Legacy `aws/deploy.sh` still describes port 8000. Compose listens on 8100. Replace that script rather than pointing it at Bedrock.

## This Mac runs the 7B trio and cannot host Luna

**Evidence.** The machine in the question is an Apple M5 Pro with 48 GB unified memory. A third-party calculator, not an Apple specification, estimates about **40 GB** usable for weights and KV cache after reserving about 8 GB for macOS ([CanItRun M5 Pro 48GB](https://canitrun.dev/gpus/m5-pro/)). Cited weight sizes are about **1.34 GB** for MedEmbed-large-v0.1, **2.27 GB** for bge-reranker-v2-m3’s safetensors, and about **13.5 GB** for MedSwin-7B-SFT in BF16. Together that is about **17 GB** of weights, inside the calculator’s usable band, with room left for KV cache and the operating system. The same calculator treats 70B-class models as needing aggressive quantisation on 48 GB. That class is outside this backbone. No published on-device benchmark of MedEmbed plus bge-reranker-v2-m3 plus MedSwin on an M5 Pro was found. Community GGUF builds of MedEmbed-large are cited around **0.8 GB** at f16 ([MedEmbed GGUF](https://huggingface.co/mradermacher/MedEmbed-large-v0.1-GGUF)). Apple’s PyTorch Metal guide requires an Apple silicon Mac, macOS 14 or later, and Python 3.10 or later ([Apple PyTorch Metal](https://developer.apple.com/metal/pytorch/)). MLX has the same operating-system floor and needs native arm64 Python ([MLX install](https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/install.html)). MPS-versus-CPU numerical parity for fp16 reranking is not documented by BAAI. GPT-6 Luna’s 1,050,000-token context and its Bedrock-only serving model are not a local checkpoint. Nothing in the notes puts Luna weights on a Mac.

The current local path will not exercise that trio even though the RAM is there. Cloud mode is on, so startup skips the download, and generation stays on Foundry. Turning cloud mode off with the code as it stands downloads MiniLM into the MedEmbed directory and mean-pools. The local HTTP ports in the audit are embeddings on 8005, rerank on 8004, and the OpenAI-shaped MedSwin server on 127.0.0.1:8000. The API itself defaults to port 8100. `scripts/start-local.sh` does not start Mongo or the local LLM. The DaRE-TIES checkpoint configured in `.env` is the one to load for a local generator test. The SFT card is the documented specification of the family. They are siblings, and the audit did not open a separate config for the merge.

**What to change.** Use this Mac as the research cell. Run MedEmbed and bge-reranker-v2-m3 on PyTorch MPS through sentence-transformers and FlagEmbedding, from the Hub weights, after the download bug is fixed. Run the 7B generator through MLX or a Metal llama.cpp / Ollama server behind the existing OpenAI-shaped local URL, with `GENERATION_BACKEND` set to the local backend. Keep `CLOUD_MODE` true only when embeddings and rerank stay on Foundry or, later, on Bedrock. Do not mix those vectors with the local 1024-d index. Quantisation is optional headroom for a crowded machine. It is not required to fit the three cited weight files. Smoke tests stay on public biomedical IR: a single MTEB medical task such as CUREv1 or NFCorpus, a monotonic rerank check on synthetic pairs with no patient data, and a latency note for K around 32 at 512 tokens versus longer pairs. Expect rerank latency, not RAM, to be the limit. The 8192-token rerank window is real, and using it on every pair will dominate a bedside loop. Cap the pair length. Keep the generator behind the gate and behind human review, which is what the MedSwin card already says. Do not describe a passed smoke test as diagnostic accuracy.

## Conclusion

The service is closest to complete where it already refuses to speak: a failed gate does not call synthesis, and the passage has to carry an id. The distance that remains is identity. The names in the environment, the vectors in the index, and the model strings on the wire each describe a system the process does not run. Lock those three to the predicates, the embedder, and the Bedrock profile you will actually call, rebuild the index once, and put rerank in a region where `cohere.rerank-v3-5:0` exists. After that, the CDS Hooks card is an adapter over a decision the code already makes, and this Mac is large enough to hold the research trio beside that cloud path. Until the vector space and the gate agree, a healthier hostname only speeds up a mismatched answer.
